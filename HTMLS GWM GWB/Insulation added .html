Why Insulation Is a Priority for Businesses

For restaurants, hotels, offices, and healthcare facilities, maintaining a stable indoor temperature is both a comfort necessity and a legal obligation. Without adequate insulation, heating and cooling systems work far harder than required, consuming more energy and wearing out sooner. A well-insulated building retains conditioned air for longer, meaning your HVAC equipment runs less frequently and your energy bills fall dramatically. UK businesses that have combined insulation upgrades with smart HVAC controls have reported combined energy savings of up to 75% compared to unimproved premises.

Types of Commercial & Domestic Insulation

Choosing the right insulation type depends on the building's construction, usage, and budget. Here are the most common solutions:

🧱 Cavity Wall Insulation

Injected or fitted into the gap between the inner and outer leaf of a cavity wall. Ideal for most post-1920s brick-built commercial and residential properties. Prevents up to 35% of heating loss through external walls.

Up to 35% heat retained

🏠 Loft & Roof Insulation

Mineral wool, rigid boards, or spray foam applied between and over rafters or joists. The most cost-effective first insulation measure—heat rises, so uninsulated roofs account for up to 25% of total heat loss in a building.

Payback in under 2 years

🪟 External Wall Insulation (EWI)

Rigid insulation boards fixed to the outside of the building and finished with a weatherproof render. Ideal for solid-wall buildings, period properties, and commercial premises that cannot use cavity wall methods. Also improves the external appearance.

Reduces heat loss by up to 45%

🏢 Internal Wall Insulation (IWI)

Insulated plasterboard or studwork fitted inside external walls. A popular choice for listed buildings and commercial refurbishments where external appearance must be preserved. Minimal disruption to operations.

U-value improved by up to 80%

🌬️ Floor Insulation

Rigid boards fitted under a new floor screed, or between suspended timber floor joists. Prevents cold air infiltrating from below, particularly in older commercial buildings with suspended floors or basements. Reduces draughts and improves occupant comfort significantly.

Reduces floor heat loss by 15–20%

💨 Spray Foam Insulation

Expanding polyurethane foam that fills every gap, crack, and cavity in awkward spaces such as roof structures, pipework voids, and service ducts. Excellent for complex commercial buildings where traditional batts cannot reach. Also acts as an air barrier.

Seals 100% of treated voids

🔗 Insulation & HVAC: The Perfect Partnership

Insulation and smart HVAC controls work best in combination. Insulation reduces the load on your heating and cooling equipment, while demand-controlled ventilation ensures the air you condition is not immediately lost through leaks or poor building fabric. Together, they deliver results neither could achieve alone.

  • Reduces peak heating & cooling demand on HVAC by up to 40%
  • Allows smaller, less expensive HVAC units to be specified
  • Maintains stable internal temperatures, improving staff comfort
  • Supports compliance with Building Regulations Part L and MEES
  • Uses ETL-listed technology with independently verified efficiency criteria
  • Contributes directly to Net Zero Carbon commitments

💶 💷 Funding & Grant Support Available

Businesses and homeowners across the UK and EU may be eligible for substantial financial support for insulation upgrades. UK schemes such as the Great British Insulation Scheme, ECO4, and local authority grants can fund all or part of the installation cost. In the Netherlands and across the EU, national energy transition subsidies (ISDE, SEEH) provide additional pathways to cost-effective investment. ETL-listed products meet independently verified efficiency criteria; check current UK tax relief with a tax adviser and ask Andrieus (Grants) about schemes. Combined with grants where eligible, the net cost of a comprehensive insulation and HVAC upgrade is often significantly lower than businesses anticipate.
