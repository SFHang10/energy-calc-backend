The Circular Economy

Materials have a life
beyond the skip.

The circular economy keeps materials in use for as long as possible — extracting maximum value, then recovering and regenerating products and materials at the end of each service life.

🔩

Materials retain value

Copper, aluminium, and steel inside old appliances can be recovered and resold — generating real income rather than landfill cost.

⚡

Recycling saves massive energy

Producing aluminium from recycled scrap uses 95% less energy than smelting bauxite ore. Copper recycling saves up to 85%.

🌍

A global responsibility

The EU's WEEE Directive and UK Resources & Waste Strategy both recognise appliance recycling as essential to meeting climate targets.

💷

Real financial reward

A single 2010-era fridge can yield £30–£65 in scrap value when metals are properly sorted. A community batch of 10 appliances can exceed £400.

Why It Matters

Six reasons recycling
makes a difference.

Every appliance properly recycled is a vote for a cleaner, more resource-efficient future.

🌱
Environmental Impact

Recycled metals require 70–90% less energy than virgin production. Every tonne of copper recycled prevents 4 tonnes of CO₂ emissions.

💼
Local Jobs

Recycling creates skilled employment in collection, dismantling, sorting, and remanufacturing — keeping economic value in communities.

⛏️
Reduced Mining

Every kg of recycled copper reduces demand for environmentally damaging mining operations, protecting fragile ecosystems worldwide.

🔄
Resource Security

Recycling reduces dependency on imported raw materials, strengthening supply chain resilience and national economic independence.

🏘️
Community Value

Community collection initiatives build social connections while generating shared financial returns for local groups and councils.

♾️
Extended Lifecycles

Appliances can be repaired, parts resold, or fully recycled. Nothing needs to be wasted. Every route extends material value.

Your Options

How to recycle —
from easy to advanced.

You don't have to strip metals yourself for appliances to be recycled. But how much you do determines how much value you capture.

A
No Effort Required

Whole-Appliance WEEE Recycling

Drop the appliance intact at a local authority WEEE point or retailer take-back scheme. Refrigerants are removed safely by law, then the unit is shredded and metals separated centrally. This is how ~90% of EU appliances are recycled. Safest and fully compliant.

🇬🇧 £0–£15 🇪🇺 €0–€20
B
Minimal Effort

Scrap Yard — Whole Appliance

Take the fridge intact to a licensed scrap yard. They'll pay a flat rate for the mixed shreddable metal. Easy and legal, though you give up the premium value of separated copper and aluminium.

🇬🇧 £5–£20 🇪🇺 €10–€25
C
Best for Individuals

Partial Dismantling (Safe DIY)

Remove external copper wiring, aluminium panels, shelves, doors — and the compressor as a sealed unit. Never open refrigerant lines yourself. Sell sorted metals at higher per-kg rates, then take the carcass to WEEE recycling. This is the sweet spot for most people.

🇬🇧 £25–£45 🇪🇺 €30–€55
D
Professional Level

Full Manual Dismantling

Certified refrigerant removal, full copper coil extraction, complete aluminium and steel separation, sorted plastics. This is what licensed repair shops and scrap traders do. Requires WEEE registration for regular operations, but delivers maximum yield.

🇬🇧 £50–£80 🇪🇺 €80–€120
E
Highest Value

Re-use Before Recycling

Working compressors, door seals, shelves, electronics, and entire refurbished units can all be resold — often commanding more than raw scrap value. Repair, test, and list on eBay, Facebook Marketplace, or local classifieds. This is the circular economy at its finest.

🇬🇧 £40–£150+ 🇪🇺 €50–€180+

Before you go

  • Scrap metal prices change daily — always call ahead for current rates
  • Separate metals before you arrive to unlock higher per-kg payouts
  • Compressors must stay sealed — refrigerant removal requires certification
  • One-off household dismantling is fine; regular selling may need WEEE registration
  • Compare at least two yards — prices vary significantly between buyers
Policy Alignment

Backed by law,
driven by purpose.

Appliance recycling sits at the heart of the major environmental frameworks shaping Europe's future.

🇪🇺

EU WEEE Directive (2012/19/EU)

Mandates the waste hierarchy — prevention, then reuse, then recycling. Sets legally binding material recovery targets and extends producer responsibility across the appliance lifecycle.

🇬🇧

UK Resources & Waste Strategy

Focuses on resource efficiency and value retention, moving towards zero avoidable waste through circular economy principles and stronger producer obligations.

🎯

EU Circular Economy Action Plan

The EU's roadmap for sustainable product design, empowering consumers, and creating functioning markets for secondary raw materials — keeping metals in circulation indefinitely.
